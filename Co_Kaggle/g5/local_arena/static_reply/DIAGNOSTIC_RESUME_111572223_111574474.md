## Existing regression semantics verified by inspection

`test_diagnose.py` already verifies several important interpretation rules:

1. A duplicate newly opened shop counts as an additional demand instance.
2. A newly placed opponent animal reduces the corresponding product gap while adding
   wheat/feed demand.
3. A positive fractional producer shortage can be blocked by `floor(gap) == 0`;
   this must not be reported as "no demand".
4. Same-species replanting is treated as a new capacity event.
5. Non-finite gaps serialize explicitly as null.

These semantics are directly relevant to the requested diagnosis.

## Important interpretation already established

Do **not** equate any of the following with economic loss without further evidence:

- gross fertilizer purchase spend;
- repeated eligible-unsent stock counted across turns;
- acquisition cost of an escaped animal with its full counterfactual lifetime profit;
- forecasted capacity with guaranteed realized production.

For selling analysis, distinguish:

- product physically in the shed and eligible to sell;
- product carried by workers and therefore not directly sellable;
- explicit wheat/fertilizer reserve withholding;
- submitted sell quantity;
- actual successful market fills;
- post-worker prediction errors;
- harvested product that reaches sale only after transport/end-of-day deposit.

### Animal/feed failure: strongest large-loss defect

Every observed animal disappearance in both games happens at an end-of-day boundary
with the animal unfed and our shed holding zero WHEAT.

There are **no `BUY_PRODUCT WHEAT` orders in either game**.

| Episode | Animal losses | Species | Acquisition cost |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| 111572223 | 16 | 13 SHEEP, 2 COW, 1 GOOSE | 7,600 |
| 111574474 | 17 | 15 COW, 1 SHEEP, 1 GOOSE | 6,800 |
| Combined | 33 | — | 14,400 |

Both games lose the same four opening animals at D1 H23 -> D2 H0:
2 COW, 1 SHEEP, 1 GOOSE. At that boundary all four are unfed and WHEAT stock is zero.

This lines up with the source architecture:

- `production_signals()` includes animal feed demand in the WHEAT production signal.
- `market_orders()` explicitly has **no `BUY_PRODUCT WHEAT` path**; shortages are
  handled by `BUY_SEED WHEAT`.
- The opening policy buys animals before any crop can possibly provide immediate feed.
- `animal_plan()` places every owned animal that fits and does not gate placement on
  current/near-term feed availability.

Therefore the current architecture reacts to urgent feed demand with a long-horizon
production response. This is the clearest cross-subsystem mismatch found so far:
`production_signals` models feed demand, but `market_orders` cannot satisfy it with
immediate WHEAT product procurement, while `animal_plan` continues deployment.

The exact score improvement still needs the existing `feed_buffer` intervention; the
14,400 acquisition coins are asset cost, not a guaranteed additive score delta.

### Opponent capacity -> our buying behavior

The source behavior is directionally clear:

- every public opponent crop/animal contributes to `production_signals()` capacity;
  therefore a new same-product opponent producer can only reduce our same-product
  production gap, all else equal;
- opponent animals also increase WHEAT/feed demand, which can increase the WHEAT signal.

Observed immediate same-producer buying on the exact turn an opponent producer appears
is rare. In the raw histories, opponent COW/SHEEP/GOOSE additions are not followed by
same-species purchases within 24 turns in the simple event-window audit. WHEAT is the
main exception: many opponent WHEAT additions occur during periods in which we also buy
WHEAT seeds later.

Do **not** interpret the 24-turn correlation as causal. The exact question "did this
opponent addition suppress a buy?" requires the existing same-observation removal probe
because many additions occur close together and other gates dominate.

### Immediate selling

For ordinary non-reserve products
(MILK, WOOL, STRAWBERRY, MELON, EGG, TOMATO, CARROT), a simple raw-history audit found
**zero turns in either episode where product was already in the shed at the pre-action
observation and the market action omitted that product entirely**.

That is strong evidence that the `market_orders()` "sell first" rule is behaving as
intended for already-shed non-reserve goods.

It does **not** prove zero delivery lag. Same-turn harvest may remain in a worker
inventory, and WHEAT/FERTILIZER have explicit reserve logic. Use `selling.csv`,
`post_action_market_inventory()`, and harvest-to-sale lag to isolate:
worker-carried inventory, prediction errors, reserve withholding, order-limit effects,
and unfilled orders.
