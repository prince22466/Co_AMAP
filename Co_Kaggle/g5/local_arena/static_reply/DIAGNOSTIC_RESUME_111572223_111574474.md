## Existing regression semantics verified by inspection

`test_diagnose.py` already verifies several important interpretation rules:

1. A duplicate newly opened shop counts as an additional demand instance.
2. A newly placed opponent animal reduces the corresponding product gap while adding
   wheat/feed demand.
3. A positive fractional producer shortage can be blocked by `floor(gap) == 0`;
   this must not be reported as "no demand".
4. Same-species replanting is treated as a new capacity event.
5. Non-finite gaps serialize explicitly as null.

These semantics are directly relevant to the requested diagnosis.

## Important interpretation already established

Do **not** equate any of the following with economic loss without further evidence:

- gross fertilizer purchase spend;
- repeated eligible-unsent stock counted across turns;
- acquisition cost of an escaped animal with its full counterfactual lifetime profit;
- forecasted capacity with guaranteed realized production.

For selling analysis, distinguish:

- product physically in the shed and eligible to sell;
- product carried by workers and therefore not directly sellable;
- explicit wheat/fertilizer reserve withholding;
- submitted sell quantity;
- actual successful market fills;
- post-worker prediction errors;
- harvested product that reaches sale only after transport/end-of-day deposit.

## Next analysis steps

When replay output is available, resume in this order:

### A. Shop opening -> signal -> procurement -> deployment

For every `shop_open` event:

- compare demand and producer-equivalent gap with/without the shop;
- identify the corresponding producer;
- record whether a buy was emitted immediately;
- if no buy, classify the exact gate:
  `gap_below_one`, cash, crop/animal space, deadline, order-slot pressure, or other;
- record first matching purchase and units within 24/72 turns;
- follow successful purchase to deployment using FIFO lag;
- follow deployed asset to harvest, sale, escape/death, or zero-harvest terminal state.

This separates a correct `production_signals` response from failures in
`market_orders`, `crop_plan`, or `animal_plan`.

### B. Opponent capacity event response

For every `opponent_capacity` event:

- quantify same-product capacity delta;
- quantify feed-demand delta for new animals;
- compare actual orders to orders with that event removed;
- determine whether our corresponding seed/animal buy was:
  - reduced as intended due to competitor supply;
  - unchanged because another gate dominated;
  - perversely increased;
- aggregate by producer type and identify the largest score-relevant cases.

Also compare the full-season `ignore_opponent_capacity` intervention. This is a
sensitivity test, not a recommendation to ignore opponent supply.

### C. Animal survival and feed path

For the four opening animals and all later animals:

- inspect daily herd size, wheat stock, and unfed count;
- find every feed action and wheat purchase;
- determine whether failure is:
  - no wheat signal/procurement;
  - wheat bought too late;
  - animal planner/scheduler not issuing feed actions;
  - worker unable to reach/execute;
  - another resource/order constraint.

Use `feed_buffer` and `feed_and_fert` outcome deltas to measure score/margin impact.

### D. Immediate selling

For each non-reserve product:

- find post-worker `eligible > requested`;
- separate predictor error from deliberate reserve withholding;
- separate shed inventory from worker-carried inventory;
- measure harvest-to-sale lag and day-boundary crossings;
- identify products with repeated unsent eligible shed stock;
- compare the `deliver_products` intervention for the transport bottleneck.

The central test is whether "sell immediately" fails at:
worker delivery, inventory prediction, reserve logic, order construction, or market fill.

### E. Fertilizer

Report together:

- units bought;
- units sold;
- units consumed;
- collected fertilizer;
- remaining/overflow;
- FIFO resale P&L;
- full `fert_no_churn` intervention effect.

Do not call gross fertilizer buying a loss if resale cash offsets it.
