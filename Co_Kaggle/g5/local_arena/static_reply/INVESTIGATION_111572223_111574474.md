- `111572223.json`
- `111574474.json`

The investigation target is to separate bad outcomes caused by:

- `production_signals`
- `animal_plan`
- `crop_plan`
- `market_orders`

and to trace event -> signal -> order/action -> realized outcome, especially:

## Confirmed findings — episode 111572223

### P1 — Critical: feed demand is signaled as WHEAT production, but procurement cannot buy feed wheat

This is the clearest causal failure found so far.

`production_signals()` explicitly adds one WHEAT unit/day for every visible animal (plus our unplaced animals) to remaining hard demand. However, `market_orders()` has **no `BUY_PRODUCT WHEAT` path**. Its own comment states that WHEAT shortages are expected to compete through normal `BUY_SEED WHEAT` producer procurement.

That couples a short-horizon consumable requirement (feed needed today / tomorrow) to a long-horizon producer purchase (seed that must be planted, maintained, and mature before yielding).

Observed opening sequence in `111572223`:

- D0 H0: fixed opening buys 13 WHEAT seeds and 2 COW.
- D0 H1: buys 1 SHEEP and 1 GOOSE.
- D0 H3..H7: `animal_plan` / worker execution successfully builds structures and places all four opening animals.
- D0 H4 through D1 H23: candidate WHEAT **product** inventory remains 0.
- There is no `BUY_PRODUCT WHEAT` order and no FEED action during this interval.
- CARE actions do occur, so this is not simply "the animal planner ignored livestock."
- At D2 H0 all four opening animals are gone.

Interpretation:

- `animal_plan` performs the opening deployment as intended.
- `production_signals` knows animals imply WHEAT demand.
- The failure is at the **signal semantics -> market procurement interface**: feed demand is converted into seed demand instead of immediate product demand.
- Because newly placed animals survive their first unfed day but escape after two consecutive unfed days, the opening wheat crop cannot mature soon enough to save them.

This should be treated as a critical market-policy defect, not primarily an `animal_plan` defect.

### P2 — Confirmed implementation defect: fertilizer is sold and repurchased in the same turn

Episode-level submitted-order counts in `111572223`:

- `BUY_PRODUCT FERTILIZER`: 2,482 units.
- `SELL FERTILIZER`: 2,518 units.
- 167 decisions contain **both** a fertilizer SELL and fertilizer BUY.

The churn starts immediately:

- D0 H5: buy 4.
- D0 H6: sell 4, buy 2.
- D0 H7: sell 2, buy 4.
- D0 H8: sell 4, buy 3.
- D0 H9: sell 3, buy 6.
- D0 H10: sell 6, buy 4.
- the same pattern continues repeatedly.

The code explains the mechanism:

1. SELL runs first.
2. Before day 10, `reserve_fert=0`, so shed fertilizer is eligible to be sold immediately.
3. The later fertilizer-buy stage sizes a target from growing crops.
4. That buy stage uses `total=totals(p)`, a snapshot from **before the same-turn sells are applied**.
5. Consequently the policy can sell fertilizer and then issue a replacement buy in the same market action list.

This is definitely bad policy behavior, but gross fertilizer purchase spend must **not** be reported as economic loss. The exact loss is the net transaction P&L / market-impact / opportunity-cost effect. The existing FIFO engine ledger remains the correct tool for quantifying that.

### P3 — Shop signals work, but same-turn procurement is often pre-empted by order priority

The first `FARMERS_MARKET` unlock is at D3 H0.

Removing just this new shop from the same observation and recomputing `production_signals()` gives these producer-equivalent shortages:

| Product | Gap without shop | Gap with shop | Immediate buy | Gate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| WHEAT | 0.000 | 4.292 | 0 | order_limit |
| CARROT | 0.000 | 5.708 | 0 | order_limit |
| TOMATO | 0.688 | 10.813 | 0 | order_limit |
| STRAWBERRY | 0.000 | 15.300 | 0 | order_limit |

So the shop event **does generate the expected signals**.

The reason nothing is bought on D3 H0 is that the action list is:

- SELL WHEAT 22
- HIRE x9

which consumes all 10 market-order slots before producer procurement.

At D3 H1, after the order-limit pressure clears, the signal ranking is:

1. STRAWBERRY gap 15.1 producers
2. TOMATO gap 10.688
3. CARROT gap 5.625
4. WHEAT gap 5.125

and the agent buys:

- STRAWBERRY seeds x3
- TOMATO seed x1
- CARROT seed x1

The remaining crop deployment space is consumed by the higher-ranked purchases, so WHEAT is not bought.

Interpretation: the first Farmers Market response is broadly working as designed. The one-turn delay is due to `market_orders` priority/order capacity, not missing shop awareness.

Later shop events show the same separation between signaling and execution:

- D6 H0 `BRUNCH_SPOT`: positive new EGG/WHEAT/STRAWBERRY shortages, but cash reserve blocks all producer buys.
- D9 H0 `YARN_STORE`: WOOL gap jumps from 0 to 8.45 SHEEP equivalents, but cash reserve blocks purchase.
- D12 H0 `PET_CAFE`: CARROT gap jumps 8.4 -> 22.8 producer equivalents; no deployment space + cash + order limit block the same-turn buy.
- D18 H0 `ICE_CREAM_SHOP`: STRAWBERRY and WHEAT shortages become positive, but crop space and order limit block same-turn procurement.
- Late shop openings additionally hit producer maturity/buy-deadline gates.

Therefore "shop opened but no purchase" is often **not** a `production_signals` failure. The diagnostic must preserve the gate reason.

### P4 — Opponent capacity is incorporated into signals, but immediate buying is usually insensitive

For `111572223`, individual opponent producer additions/replantings were removed one at a time from the current observation and signals recomputed. The producer-buy loop was independently reconstructed and matches the recorded producer orders on 717 / 718 checked decisions.

Observed opponent capacity additions:

- COW: 9
- SHEEP: 5
- GOOSE: 3
- WHEAT crop/replant events: 162
- CARROT: 31
- STRAWBERRY: 33
- MELON: 12

The signal layer behaves directionally as intended:

- same-product public capacity can reduce the corresponding producer-equivalent shortage;
- opponent animal additions also increase modeled WHEAT/feed demand.

But the **immediate producer purchase list changed for only one isolated opponent-capacity event**:

- D15 H10: one opponent WHEAT production event changed WHEAT gap from 1.583 producer equivalents without the event to 0.500 with it, suppressing a one-seed WHEAT purchase.

All other isolated opponent producer additions left the immediate same-producer purchase quantity unchanged because the changed signal did not cross the integer `floor(gap)` purchase boundary or another cash/space/order/deadline gate dominated.

Interpretation:

- opponent supply is not being ignored;
- its effect on immediate buying is heavily quantized by `floor(producer_equivalent_gap)` and procurement gates;
- the larger concern is whether the **capacity model itself is too optimistic**, especially because `_current_crop_remaining_capacity()` assumes current crops will be replanted repeatedly with the same crop after their last harvest, including opponent crops. The existing `no_repeat_capacity` intervention is the correct next causal test for score impact.
