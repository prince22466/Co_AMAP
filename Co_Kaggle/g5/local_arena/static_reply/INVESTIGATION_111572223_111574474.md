## Confirmed findings — episode 111572223

### P1 — Critical: feed demand is signaled as WHEAT production, but procurement cannot buy feed wheat

This is the clearest causal failure found so far.

`production_signals()` explicitly adds one WHEAT unit/day for every visible animal (plus our unplaced animals) to remaining hard demand. However, `market_orders()` has **no `BUY_PRODUCT WHEAT` path**. Its own comment states that WHEAT shortages are expected to compete through normal `BUY_SEED WHEAT` producer procurement.

That couples a short-horizon consumable requirement (feed needed today / tomorrow) to a long-horizon producer purchase (seed that must be planted, maintained, and mature before yielding).

Observed opening sequence in `111572223`:

- D0 H0: fixed opening buys 13 WHEAT seeds and 2 COW.
- D0 H1: buys 1 SHEEP and 1 GOOSE.
- D0 H3..H7: `animal_plan` / worker execution successfully builds structures and places all four opening animals.
- D0 H4 through D1 H23: candidate WHEAT **product** inventory remains 0.
- There is no `BUY_PRODUCT WHEAT` order and no FEED action during this interval.
- CARE actions do occur, so this is not simply "the animal planner ignored livestock."
- At D2 H0 all four opening animals are gone.

Interpretation:

- `animal_plan` performs the opening deployment as intended.
- `production_signals` knows animals imply WHEAT demand.
- The failure is at the **signal semantics -> market procurement interface**: feed demand is converted into seed demand instead of immediate product demand.
- Because newly placed animals survive their first unfed day but escape after two consecutive unfed days, the opening wheat crop cannot mature soon enough to save them.

This should be treated as a critical market-policy defect, not primarily an `animal_plan` defect.

### P2 — Confirmed implementation defect: fertilizer is sold and repurchased in the same turn

Episode-level submitted-order counts in `111572223`:

- `BUY_PRODUCT FERTILIZER`: 2,482 units.
- `SELL FERTILIZER`: 2,518 units.
- 167 decisions contain **both** a fertilizer SELL and fertilizer BUY.

The churn starts immediately:

- D0 H5: buy 4.
- D0 H6: sell 4, buy 2.
- D0 H7: sell 2, buy 4.
- D0 H8: sell 4, buy 3.
- D0 H9: sell 3, buy 6.
- D0 H10: sell 6, buy 4.
- the same pattern continues repeatedly.

The code explains the mechanism:

1. SELL runs first.
2. Before day 10, `reserve_fert=0`, so shed fertilizer is eligible to be sold immediately.
3. The later fertilizer-buy stage sizes a target from growing crops.
4. That buy stage uses `total=totals(p)`, a snapshot from **before the same-turn sells are applied**.
5. Consequently the policy can sell fertilizer and then issue a replacement buy in the same market action list.

This is definitely bad policy behavior, but gross fertilizer purchase spend must **not** be reported as economic loss. The exact loss is the net transaction P&L / market-impact / opportunity-cost effect. The existing FIFO engine ledger remains the correct tool for quantifying that.

## Verified findings — episode 111572223

### Problem 1: feed demand is modeled, but urgent feed procurement is impossible

This is now verified directly from the recorded episode and the v20_3 policy.

Policy structure:

- `_remaining_hard_demand()` adds one WHEAT/day for every visible animal, so feed demand does enter `production_signals()`.
- WHEAT maps to producer `WHEAT`, therefore the signal-driven procurement path can only emit `BUY_SEED WHEAT`.
- `market_orders()` has no `BUY_PRODUCT WHEAT` path. Its only `BUY_PRODUCT` logic is fertilizer.
- Therefore a WHEAT/feed shortage can trigger more future wheat production but cannot buy immediately consumable wheat.

Observed in `111572223`:

- End of day 0: 4 animals present (2 COW, 1 SHEEP, 1 GOOSE), all 4 `fed_today=false`, all 4 `cared_today=true`.
- End of day 0: total owned WHEAT = 0; FEED actions during the day = 0.
- End of day 1: the same 4 animals are still present, all 4 unfed and cared; total owned WHEAT = 0; FEED actions = 0.
- At D1 H23 all four disappear from the farm while still `fed_today=false` and `cared_today=true`.
- Across the episode, submitted `BUY_PRODUCT WHEAT` quantity = **0**.
- Across the episode, submitted `BUY_SEED WHEAT` quantity = **68**.

Interpretation:

- `animal_plan` successfully places/retains the opening livestock.
- The worker scheduler is not the primary cause for these four opening losses because there is no wheat available to schedule FEED with.
- The dominant failure is the interface between `production_signals` and `market_orders`: a short-horizon consumption need is represented as long-horizon production-capacity shortage.
- Buying WHEAT seeds cannot save animals that need feed before the crop matures.
- This is a genuine policy-path defect, not merely bad signal ranking.

Economic impact already visible from the four opening animals: acquisition cost is 2×400 + 500 + 300 = **1,600 coins**, excluding lost future production.

This should be tested causally with the existing `feed_buffer` intervention.


### Problem 2: animal placement is feed-blind and amplifies the procurement defect

`animal_plan()` deliberately plans every owned animal onto an unlocked route slot and does not gate placement on:

- WHEAT currently available,
- near-term WHEAT production,
- ability to buy feed,
- number of workers able to feed/care the resulting herd.

That behavior is visible in both histories.

### Finding 4: opponent supply is included and generally suppresses duplicate producer buying

The signal model counts both players' visible current assets and ready yield as production capacity.

In `111572223`, direct inspection of opponent producer additions shows:

- new opponent COW/SHEEP/GOOSE capacity does not trigger same-producer purchases by us in the following 24 turns in the sampled events;
- new opponent animal capacity also increases our modeled WHEAT/feed demand, because `_remaining_hard_demand()` counts visible animals on **both** farms as WHEAT consumers;
- typical isolated opponent-animal additions raised the WHEAT producer-equivalent gap by roughly 0.7–0.8 while reducing or leaving unchanged the corresponding MILK/WOOL/EGG gap.

This means the competitor-response mechanism is directionally working:

- opponent product supply reduces our need to duplicate that product capacity;
- opponent animals simultaneously increase modeled public feed demand.

However, the latter interacts badly with Problem 1: extra feed demand raises the WHEAT **seed** signal, while urgent WHEAT product procurement is absent.

Further quantification of opponent-capacity events remains useful, but no evidence so far suggests that failure to observe opponent supply is the major loss driver.

### Finding 6: fertilizer churn is real, but gross turnover must not be called the cash loss

Submitted fertilizer flow:

- `111572223`: BUY_PRODUCT FERTILIZER **2,482 units**, SELL FERTILIZER **2,518 units**, with **167 turns** containing both buy and sell fertilizer orders.
- `111574474`: BUY_PRODUCT FERTILIZER **3,462 units**, SELL FERTILIZER **3,469 units**, with **224 turns** containing both buy and sell fertilizer orders.

The oscillation starts immediately. Example from 111572223:

- D0 H6: SELL 4, BUY 2
- D0 H7: SELL 2, BUY 4
- D0 H8: SELL 4, BUY 3
- D0 H9: SELL 3, BUY 6
- then similar alternation continues.

Do **not** estimate fertilizer loss as gross buy spend minus a naive fixed-price sale estimate. The game rules price buys/sells against changing shared inventory one unit at a time; offsetting same-turn round trips can net approximately/exactly zero depending on order interaction.

What is already clearly harmful even before exact FIFO cash attribution:

1. massive unnecessary order churn;
2. fertilizer SELL orders consume high-priority market-order slots before producer procurement;
3. buy/sell oscillation increases cash/order-state sensitivity;
4. it creates noise in any gross-spend diagnosis.

The existing engine-level FIFO ledger in `summarize_diagnostics.py` remains the correct way to assign actual fertilizer P&L. The `fert_no_churn` intervention is the correct causal score test.
